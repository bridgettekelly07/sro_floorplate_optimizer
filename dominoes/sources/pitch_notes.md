# Pitch notes

## The tenant sentence

Goes in the Problem section, after the as-is diagram.

Every actor in the process produces a document: the owner an application, Council a permit with conditions, the architect drawings, the funder an agreement, the City a survey. The tenant produces none and receives none. The relocation plan is written about them, reviewed without them, and never followed up. The tool is the first point in the sequence where the tenant's timeline is drawn.

Shorter, for the 150 words: The tenant is the only party in the process who produces no document and receives none; the tool draws their timeline for the first time.

## Two findings from the permit files that back it

Low Young Court, 2015. The owner filed the SRA application on April 29 and the last tenants were out on April 30. The relocation plan was reviewed by Council twelve weeks after the relocation it describes. Source: council.vancouver.ca/20150721/documents/a3.pdf.

Old Continental, 2013. Replacement was committed in policy in 2010. Tenants moved to interim housing outside the neighbourhood in summer 2013, 52 of 85. The report gives no date for the permanent replacement. Three years to an interim move, and an open end. Source: council.vancouver.ca/20130227/documents/cfsc6.pdf.

Use one of these in the pitch, not both. Low Young Court is the sharper one for the tenant sentence. Old Continental is the one for the fee-to-replacement lag if a reviewer asks why the tool needs years.

## Users, in session order

Session 1, by Nov 12 or 16: a non-profit SRO operator. They hold a real building file with dates, which the as-is axis needs, and they can run the tool on a renovation they have actually done. Candidates: Atira, PHS, Lookout, or an operator CCAP or the SRO Collaborative can name.

Session 2, by Nov 27: a City housing planner. They make the decisions in `edges.json` and can test the compare loop on an option they have chosen between.

Session 3, by Dec 3: a tenant group or advocate, such as the SRO Collaborative or CCAP. They have no instrument in the process; the session tests whether the forks read as fair to the party the tool was built to draw.

State in the proposal that the user you name for Nov 6 is the operator, and that the other two are planned.
