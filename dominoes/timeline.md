Every date and figure below comes from a document in `sources/`, with the page it stands on. Page numbers
are **PDF pages**; the DTES Plan's printed page number is one less (PDF p.100 is printed p.99). Anything
that is the tool's own arithmetic or inference is marked **[derived]**; anything the sources do not say is
under *Gaps*.

## 3. The stock through time (Figure 9.3, "DTES Total Housing Stock, 2003–2043")

Unit counts, read from the chart on PDF p.100. The 2024 column is the City's reported actual; its note says
it includes temporary modular housing in operation as of 2024; the two forecasts "reflect change in housing
stock based on the 2013 stock and the housing targets set in the plan." Each column sums to its printed total.

| Type | 2003 | 2013 | 2024 actual | 10-yr forecast | 30-yr forecast (2043) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Social housing: SROs | 2,000 | 3,000 | 3,200 | 2,800 | 2,600 |
| Social housing: units | 3,200 | 4,000 | 5,800 | 5,400 | 8,400 |
| **Private SROs** | **4,800** | **3,800** | **2,700** | **2,800** | **600** |
| Secured rental | 700 | 800 | 1,400 | 2,400 | 3,800 |
| Ownership | 1,300 | 3,800 | 5,000 | 6,500 | 12,700 |
| Total | 12,000 | 15,400 | 18,100 | 19,900 | 28,100 |

Reading it against the plan's 10-year objectives (PDF p.98):

- **Private SROs**: 2,700 in 2024 against a 10-year forecast of 2,800. On the forecast.
- **Social housing units**: 4,000 to 5,800 is +1,800 against a 10-year objective of 1,400 new units. The 2024 figure includes temporary modular housing, so it is not like for like.
- **Secured rental**: 800 to 1,400 is +600 against a forecast of 2,400 (objective 1,650 new units). Behind.
- **Ownership**: 5,000 against a forecast of 6,500. Behind.
- **Total**: 18,100 against a forecast of 19,900.

### Pace of private SRO loss **[derived]**

| Period | Change | Per year |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2003 to 2013 | 4,800 to 3,800 = −1,000 | about −100 |
| 2013 to 2024 | 3,800 to 2,700 = −1,100 | about −100 |
| 2024 to 2043 (needed to reach the forecast) | 2,700 to 600 = −2,100 over 19 years | about −110 |

The 2005 Housing Plan's rate was 100 units a year (PDF p.94). The recorded pace has matched it for two decades;
the forecast asks for a slightly higher pace for the next 19, and the plan says it depends on senior-government
funding (PDF p.94, p.102).

## 4. What the plan says about money and partners

- "The City can only achieve a fraction of these ambitious goals without partners in senior government." (PDF p.102)
- The 4,400-unit social housing objective rests on three City-owned sites, replacing 200 City-owned SROs, 400 units under construction and 460 more likely in 5 to 10 years (PDF p.102).
- Replacing private SROs at 100 units a year is stated "assuming the availability of senior government funding" (PDF p.94).
- Upgraded SRA rooms: a target of one-third at the shelter rate, one-third at no more than average SRO rent, one-third above it (PDF p.98).
- Housing review clause: the density-for-social-housing exchange "may be subject to review as economic conditions change" (Policy 9.2.8, PDF p.103).

## 5. What this opens for the tool

- **A horizon the tool can show.** Private SROs 4,800 to 2,700 to 600 is one line with a 2024 mark on it, and the City's own forecast beside it.
- **A pace.** About 100 a year so far, about 110 needed. This is the "conversion rate" the tool does not yet have, taken from the City's figures, not invented.
- **A thing to compare.** The tool's district tally (532 private-stock tenants displaced at the City's thresholds) can be set against the 2,100 private SRO units the forecast says go by 2043. The two counts do not mean the same thing (see *Gaps*), so the comparison is a question, not a result.
- **Vacancy as a variable.** Vacancy Control (2024) and the survey's tenancy figures (27% moved in the past year in 2024, 29% in 2013; time at address "relatively unchanged" between 2013 and 2024; Survey, PDF p.30, p.33) are the only sourced basis for letting the tool's all-rooms-occupied assumption vary.

## 6. Gaps and cautions

1. **The chart and the survey count different things.** Figure 9.3 gives 2,700 private SRO *units* in 2024. The survey covers 76 market SRO buildings with 3,083 *rooms* (PDF p.21), and Appendix B gives the tool its 143 buildings and 6,153 rooms. The definitions and dates differ and the sources do not reconcile them.
2. **The Dec 2025 amendments are known only as resolutions.** The appendices holding the text are not in `sources/`. Whether they changed s.4.8 compensation, the social housing definition (20% shelter rate, 10% at HILs, PDF p.97 and p.103) or the thresholds in the Upgrade Guidelines is unknown. The Mar 31, 2026 plan and the Feb 3, 2026 Guidelines still carry the 200 SF and 50% figures.
3. **Which guideline.** The Dec 2025 recommendation H amends the Guidelines for the *Upgrade* of rooms; the tool uses the Guidelines for *Converting* rooms. They are different documents.
4. **No 2024 actuals for several 10-year objectives.** The plan sets 1,900 SRO upgrades in the first 10 years, 1,650 rent subsidies and 1,300 scattered sites (PDF p.98), and the sources in hand do not report how many were delivered.
5. **Figure 9.3 was read from an image.** The values were read off the chart and each column checks against its printed total, but the underlying table is not in the document.
6. **Dates of the City's monitoring reports are not in `sources/`.** The plan promises regular monitoring against 2013 indicators (PDF p.200); the reports themselves would give interim points between 2013 and 2024.
7. **The 2043 horizon, not 2044.** An earlier note in this project took the horizon as 2044; the plan's own Action Plan runs 2013–2043.
8. **The debate figures are spoken.** Section 7 has not been checked against the City update the moderator read from, and the debate's date is taken from the project's author.

### A threshold that has changed

Anton gave the SRA By-law's payment as "two hundred and thirty thousand dollars a room" (32:35). The
consolidated By-law, to Feb 3, 2026, reads at s.4.8(a): Council or the General Manager may require "$300,000
for each designated room," into a reserve fund for replacement accommodation, "but only if the designated room
ceases to be a designated room and is otherwise not replaced by the owner" (By-law, PDF p.10 to 11). So the
amount in the text now is not the amount she quoted. When it changed, and whether $230,000 was ever the
enacted figure, is not in the sources in hand.

The speakers are advocates, planners, a former minister and a housing provider. Treat each figure as a lead to
