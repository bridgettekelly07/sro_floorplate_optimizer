SRA Conversion Optimizer

A study tool for SRO conversion in the Downtown Eastside at the scale of the district. The stock is mapped building by building; each building gets a typical floor read from its own footprint, and a search over every scheme of adjacent merges finds the one that displaces the fewest tenants while passing the City’s tests. The district scenario then asks how far a conversion mandate can reach, and who it moves. Every threshold is read from the source documents and reported with its citation.

What happens
Assumptions behind the numbers

The mandate is met by choosing which buildings convert, and how far each goes, so that the fewest tenants lose their room; a building converts at a scheme of its own or not at all, since a partial conversion below 50% fails DTES 9.2.7. A building edited in section 02 is taken exactly as drawn.

Works empty a building (s.4.8(f)). Converting removes homes, so tenants can only move between buildings where there is slack: vacant rooms, homes a converted building has to spare, and new supply. The ledger follows every tenant, nearest slack first, then the relocation housing; those who cannot return are placed for good the same way, and the rest leave the district. Buildings whose conversion adds slack go first, then the gentlest. Vacancy, relocation housing and new supply are assumptions the sources do not give.

The Guidelines allow an average of 200 SF across converted rooms, at the City’s discretion; strict is the safe reading.

Room size is read from each footprint: its area less the circulation share, divided by the rooms Appendix B counts on a floor, and capped where a footprint holds far more than its count suggests, since the City counts the designated rooms and not the commercial or common floor around them.

Tenants are rooms less the vacancy assumed. The survey’s average tenancy of 4.6 years places everyone who cannot return in the 4-month bracket of s.4.8(i).

Matched against the hundred-block index

Blank is recorded as unknown, never as zero. Floorplate SF, $/SF and tenancy are not published per building by the City; enter them only where you have measured or surveyed them.

Scope. The tool evaluates a scheme you propose, and it searches for the compliant scheme of adjacent merges that loses the fewest rooms, building by building and across the district. It does not model the permit process, financial-viability findings, rent-setting, or whether a merge is physically buildable. The model is diagrammatic: rooms are single-loaded off one corridor at an assumed 12′ depth, and width follows area. Partitions are drawn at 3′ so the plan reads from above. Pod program comes from SRA Guidelines p.5–6, which dimensions only the refrigerator (24″×24″); the bathroom (5′×8′) and kitchen run (8′×2′) are conventional minimums stated here as assumptions.
