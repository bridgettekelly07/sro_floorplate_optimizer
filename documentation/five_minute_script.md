# Five minutes, three scenarios

A script for presenting the tool with the three scenario buttons above the sliders. Every figure is
what the tool shows under that button, applied to all 143 SROs in Appendix B. The lines in quotation
marks are what to say; the indented lines are what to do.

Before you start: open the page in light theme, press **Fit**, make sure **The City's rules** is the
highlighted scenario, and no building is selected. The readout under the title on the map carries
the district's figures and the bar of where every tenant goes; it updates with every scenario.

---

## 0:00 · The question

> "Vancouver lets an owner merge single-room-occupancy rooms into self-contained units if three
> tests pass: every unit reaches 200 square feet, no more than half the rooms are lost, and at
> least half come back as units. Every merge removes a room, and the tenant in it. This tool asks
> one question: under these thresholds, who leaves?"

- Gesture at the map. The coloured buildings are the 143 SROs. Yellow is a building where nobody
  is displaced, then amber, orange and red for 1 to 10, 11 to 25, and 26 or more tenants. Slate
  blue is a building that cannot convert at all.

## 0:45 · Scenario 1: The City's rules

- Point at the readout under the title.

> "This is the policy as written. 130 of 142 buildings convert. 3,786 units are delivered. And
> 1,112 tenants lose their room, 18 percent of everyone in the stock. Twelve buildings, holding 540
> rooms, cannot convert at all. The compensation owed under the by-law's own schedule is about
> 2.35 million dollars."

- Point at the bar: 1,112 displaced, 3,786 re-housed in a new unit, 702 staying in a kept room.

> "Why so many? Most rooms here are 100 to 130 square feet. No room reaches 200 on its own, so a
> unit is two rooms knocked together, and each pair sends one tenant away."

## 1:45 · One building: the Ivanhoe Hotel

- Open **Which buildings** in section 02 and click **Ivanhoe Hotel**, or click it on the map at the
  east end of False Creek, by Science World. Scroll up to the plan.

> "The Ivanhoe, 1038 Main Street. 92 rooms, about 116 square feet each, heritage listed. The plan
> is drawn at the scheme that passes the City's tests while losing the fewest rooms: 48 units, 8
> rooms kept, 36 tenants displaced. Thirty-nine percent of the building."

- Read the highlighted sentence under the tally aloud. It says the same thing in one line.

## 2:30 · Scenario 2: Smaller units

- Click **Smaller units**. The plan redraws, the map recolours, the readout updates.

> "Change one number. Units of 150 square feet instead of 200. In the Ivanhoe most rooms now
> convert in place: 52 units, 24 kept, and 16 tenants displaced instead of 36."

- Point at the readout.

> "Across the district: 136 buildings convert, 5,043 units, and 369 tenants displaced instead of
> 1,112. Six percent instead of eighteen. Fifty square feet is seven hundred and forty people."

## 3:30 · Scenario 3: Bigger units, tighter cap

- Click **Bigger units, tighter cap**.

> "Now the other direction, which is what a stricter reading of the policy looks like: 250 square
> foot units and no more than 30 percent of rooms lost. The Ivanhoe turns blue. No scheme passes,
> so it does not convert and nobody is displaced there. But look at the district."

- The readout again.

> "Only 74 of 142 buildings convert. 68 cannot, holding 3,367 rooms, more than half the stock. The
> units delivered fall to 1,641. And 690 people are still displaced, from the buildings that do
> pass. A stricter policy is not a gentler one. It protects by freezing."

## 4:30 · Landing

- Click **The City's rules** to return.

> "Three things the thresholds say on their own. The minimum unit size decides how many rooms a
> unit swallows, and that, more than anything else in the provision, decides who leaves. At 200
> square feet a room under 100 has no compliant conversion, whatever the cap, so for those buildings
> the policy means replacement, not conversion. And the stock with the smallest rooms is where any
> version of this policy costs the most. The tool does not say what the numbers should be. It shows
> what each set costs, in people, in a building you can name."

## If asked

- **Rooms lost versus tenants displaced.** Every room is taken as occupied, so they are one
  figure here. The by-law requires relocation housing and a first right of refusal on the new
  units, so permanent displacement is likely lower by a margin the sources do not give.
- **Rooms under 200 SF passing the size test.** The Guidelines allow an average of 200 SF across
  all converted rooms where 200 each cannot be achieved. The tool reads the test on that average.
- **Where the room sizes come from.** The City's footprint, less a circulation share, divided among
  the rooms Appendix B counts on a floor. The plan is a type, not a survey, and says so.
- **Public and private.** The policy is applied to every SRO in Appendix B. Of the 1,112 displaced
  at the City's rules, 532 are in the 76 private SROs and 580 in public stock.
